1. Semrush — Best Overall for Small Business Owners

If you only pick one tool from this list, this is the one most small business owners land on. Semrush combines keyword research, competitor analysis, site audits, and rank tracking in a single dashboard — which matters when you don't have a dedicated SEO hire and need one login to check everything.

What makes it worth the price:

  • Competitor gap analysis: see exactly which keywords your competitors rank for that you don't, so you're not guessing at content ideas
  • Site audit tool: flags technical issues (broken links, slow pages, missing meta tags) in plain language, not just a wall of jargon
  • Local SEO toolkit: useful if you're a service business trying to rank in a specific city or region
  • 7-day free trial: enough time to run a full audit of your own site before committing

Who it's a good fit for: small business owners and freelancers who want one tool to replace five separate subscriptions, and agencies managing SEO for multiple clients.

Who might skip it: if you only need basic keyword research once a month, the price may be more than you need — see Ubersuggest below.

2. Ahrefs — Best for Backlink Research

Ahrefs has one of the largest backlink databases in the industry, which makes it the go-to when you need to understand why a competitor outranks you — not just that they do. The content gap tool is particularly useful for finding topics you haven't covered yet.

Good for: content-heavy sites and bloggers who care most about link-building and content strategy.

3. Ubersuggest — Best Budget Option

Built by Neil Patel's team, Ubersuggest strips SEO tools down to the essentials — keyword research, basic site audits, and content ideas — at a fraction of the cost of Semrush or Ahrefs. It won't replace an enterprise toolkit, but for a solo blogger or a business just starting to take SEO seriously, it covers the fundamentals.

Good for: anyone testing whether SEO tools are worth paying for at all before committing to a bigger platform.

4. Google Search Console — Best Free Tool (No Excuses)

This one is free, made by Google, and every website owner should have it connected regardless of what paid tool they use. It won't do competitor research, but it tells you exactly how Google sees your site: which queries you already rank for, which pages have indexing issues, and your click-through rates by page.

Good for: literally everyone. There's no reason not to have this connected.

5. Screaming Frog — Best for Technical Audits

A desktop tool that crawls your site the way Google's bots do, surfacing broken links, duplicate content, missing alt text, and redirect chains. The free version covers up to 500 URLs, which is plenty for most small business sites.

Good for: anyone who wants a one-time technical health check without a monthly subscription.

Which One Should You Actually Choose?

  • Want one tool that does almost everything? → Semrush
  • Care most about backlinks and content strategy? → Ahrefs
  • On a tight budget but still want real keyword data? → Ubersuggest
  • Just want to know how Google already sees your site, for free? → Google Search Console
  • Need a one-time technical audit, not another subscription? → Screaming Frog

For most small business owners without a dedicated SEO person, starting with Semrush's free trial and layering in Google Search Console (which costs nothing) covers the vast majority of what you actually need.

FAQ

Do I need to pay for an SEO tool to rank on Google?
No — Google Search Console is free and gives you real data about your own site. Paid tools like Semrush add competitor research and automation on top of that.

What's the difference between Semrush and Ahrefs?
Both are strong all-in-one platforms. Semrush tends to have a broader feature set (including local SEO and PPC research), while Ahrefs is generally considered stronger specifically for backlink data.

Is a 7-day free trial enough time to evaluate a tool like Semrush?
It's enough to run a full site audit and a competitor gap analysis, which is usually enough to see whether the tool fits your workflow before you're charged.
