What makes it worth considering:

  • Visual automation workflows on the Marketer plan: trigger emails based on what a contact actually does (opens, clicks, purchases, abandoned carts)
  • Built-in webinar hosting for up to 100 attendees, useful for service businesses that sell through live demos or trainings

Who it's a good fit for: small businesses running abandoned-cart sequences, webinar-based sales, or automation flows more advanced than a basic welcome sequence.

Who might skip it: if you just need to send a simple monthly newsletter, this is more platform than you need — see Mailchimp or Kit below.

3. Brevo — Best Value with CRM and SMS Included

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) prices by email volume rather than contact count, which works in your favor if you have a large list you email infrequently. It also bundles in a basic CRM and SMS marketing at no extra cost — features that are usually a separate paid add-on elsewhere.

Good for: small businesses with a sizable contact list who don't email daily, and anyone who wants SMS and basic CRM without paying for a second tool.

4. Mailchimp — Best-Known, Easiest to Start

Mailchimp is still the name most people recognize first, and its drag-and-drop editor remains one of the easiest to pick up with zero learning curve. The tradeoff: its free plan has shrunk considerably in recent years, and automation features are increasingly reserved for paid tiers.

Good for: first-time email marketers who want the most beginner-friendly interface and don't mind starting on a paid plan once their list grows past a couple hundred contacts.

5. Kit (formerly ConvertKit) — Best for Creators and Newsletter Writers

Kit is built specifically for people monetizing an audience directly — through paid newsletters, digital products, or online courses — rather than businesses selling physical goods or services. Its free plan is generous by list size (up to 10,000 subscribers) but limited in features (one automation, one landing page, one form).

Good for: solo creators, bloggers, and course sellers who want a large free tier and don't need complex multi-step automation right away.

6. ActiveCampaign — Best for Advanced Automation

When your automation needs outgrow a simple welcome sequence — multi-branch logic, lead scoring, a built-in CRM tracking every contact's history — ActiveCampaign is usually where businesses land. It consistently ranks well in independent email deliverability testing, which matters once your list is large enough that inbox placement affects revenue directly.

Good for: growing businesses that need email marketing and light CRM functionality in the same platform, and are willing to pay for the added sophistication.

FAQ

Do I need a paid plan to start email marketing?
No. Systeme.io, Mailchimp, and Kit all offer usable free plans. The right time to upgrade is when you hit a contact limit or need automation features the free tier doesn't include.

What's the difference between an autoresponder and marketing automation?
An autoresponder sends a fixed sequence of emails on a schedule (day 1, day 3, day 7). Marketing automation triggers emails based on behavior — what a contact clicks, buys, or ignores — which requires a more advanced plan on most platforms.

Is it worth switching email tools once I've already started?
Usually not just to save a few dollars a month — migrating contacts, rebuilding automations, and reconnecting integrations takes real time. It's worth switching only when your current tool is missing a feature you actually need (like automation or funnel building), not for price alone.
