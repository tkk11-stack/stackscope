How to Do a Free SEO Audit of Your Own Website (No Paid Tools Required)

You don't need a $130/month subscription to find out why your site isn't ranking. Most of what a basic SEO audit uncovers — broken links, missing tags, slow pages, indexing issues — is visible with tools that cost nothing. Here's how to do it yourself in under an hour.

Step 1: Check What Google Actually Sees

Open Google Search Console (free, just needs a Google account) and check three things first:

  • Coverage report: pages Google has indexed vs. pages it's excluded, and why
  • Performance report: which queries are already bringing you impressions and clicks
  • Core Web Vitals: whether your pages pass Google's speed and stability thresholds

If you've never connected Search Console, do this before anything else on this list — everything downstream is more useful once you can see real data instead of guessing.

Step 2: Crawl Your Own Site

Download Screaming Frog (free for up to 500 URLs) and run it against your domain. It will surface:

  • Broken internal and external links (404s)
  • Missing or duplicate title tags and meta descriptions
  • Pages missing H1 tags, or with multiple H1s
  • Images missing alt text
  • Redirect chains (page A → B → C instead of A → C directly)

This is the single highest-value free step in a self-audit. Most sites have at least a handful of these issues without realizing it.

Step 3: Test Your Page Speed

Run your homepage and your most important pages through PageSpeed Insights. Pay attention to:

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): how long the main content takes to load
  • Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS): whether elements jump around as the page loads
  • Mobile score specifically — Google indexes your mobile version first

Common fixes at this stage: compress images, remove unused JavaScript, and avoid loading fonts that block rendering.

Step 4: Check Your Content Against What's Actually Ranking

Search your primary keyword in an incognito window and look at what's currently on page one. Ask yourself honestly:

  • Is your content as thorough as theirs, or thinner?
  • Are you answering the question the searcher actually has, or just mentioning the keyword?
  • Do the top results have something (data, examples, structure) that yours doesn't?

This step is manual and a little humbling, but it's free and often more useful than any tool.

Step 5: Verify Mobile Usability

Use Search Console's mobile usability report, or just open your site on your own phone. Check for text too small to read, buttons too close together, and content that requires horizontal scrolling to view properly — that last one is easy to miss on desktop but frustrating on mobile.

When Free Tools Start to Run Out of Runway

This process gets you most of the way for a single site. Where it breaks down is competitor research at scale — seeing exactly which keywords a competitor ranks for that you don't, tracking rank changes over time automatically, or auditing dozens of pages without manually clicking through each one. FAQ

How often should I re-run this audit?
Every few months, or immediately after any site redesign, platform migration, or major content update.

Can I do this on a brand new site with no traffic yet?
Yes — steps 2, 3, and 5 don't require any existing traffic. Search Console data will just be sparse until Google has crawled you a few times.

Is a free audit as good as a paid tool's audit?
For a single site, largely yes. Paid tools add automation, historical tracking, and competitor comparison — not fundamentally different checks.
